# PRD - VIEWPOINT (Unity rewrite)

Product requirements document. Version 1.0, 2026-09-03.

This document is the contract for `games/viewpoint_unity`: a complete rewrite,
in Unity, of the Godot 4 game `games/viewpoint` (state of the working tree on
2026-09-03: catalog of 8 photos, 25 levels, in-game camera, rewind, steel and
lead, level 13 rebuilt on the double flight of stairs). It was extracted from
the original source, its three design documents (`docs/PRD.md`,
`docs/SPEC_V4.md`, `docs/LEVELS_V2.md`), its unit suites, its integration probe
and its level design audit. Where the original documents and the original code
disagree, this document follows the code, and says so.

The goal is the same game: same rules, same numbers, same 25 levels, same
strings shown to the player, same verification harness. Only the engine
changes. Anything not covered here is to be resolved by reading the Godot
source, never by inventing.

Companion data files, exported verbatim from the Godot source:

- `docs/data/photos.json` : the photo catalog (section 6.3).
- `docs/data/levels.json` : the 25 levels and the decor islands (section 13).

## 1. Vision and scope

### 1.1 Pitch

VIEWPOINT is a first person 3D puzzle game inspired by the central mechanic of
Viewfinder (Sad Owl Studios, 2023). The player finds polaroids in the world,
holds one up, and PLACES it: the content of the photo materializes as real 3D
geometry, exactly where the picture showed it, from the exact point of view of
the player. Placing a photo REPLACES everything caught in its frame, up to the
photo's own depth, by the photo content. An empty photo of sky pierces walls,
floors and cages. From level 16 on, the player also carries a camera and takes
their own photos of the world.

Twenty-five short levels of floating islands in a pastel sky. Every level is
left through a teleporter that demands batteries: found in the world,
duplicated by placing a photo that contains one, freed from cages, or copied
with the camera. The three last lessons of the game are refusals: steel cages
no photo breaks, leaden batteries no film prints, and gravity that makes a
placed crate fall.

No binary asset: geometry, materials, textures (gradients, polaroid frames,
photo pictures), sky and UI are generated by code.

### 1.2 What "the same game" means

Parity is measured, not felt. The Unity build is done when:

- the 8 catalog photos and the 25 levels are the ones in `docs/data`, loaded
  from those files, with no number changed;
- every rule in sections 5 to 11 holds with the constants written there;
- every string of section 12 is shown where the original shows it;
- the ported unit suites (section 17.2) are green and cover the same
  behaviors as the 71 original test methods (1185 checks on 2026-09-03);
- the ported smoke probe (section 17.3) is green on the same scenario;
- the ported design audit (section 17.4) reports zero defects on the 25
  levels, as the original does on 2026-09-03;
- the game is playable end to end with mouse and keyboard, and the reference
  moments (section 14.6) look like the Godot renders.

### 1.3 Out of scope

- New levels, new photos, new mechanics. (The original reserves two ideas that
  stay reserved here: black and white photos that drain color from the world,
  and music or ambient sound. There is no audio in the game at all.)
- Gamepad support, localization to other languages, accessibility options.
- Any online feature.
- The legacy 3D "ghost" preview and the `Materials.ghost` variant. They are
  dead code in the original since v4 (the raised 2D picture replaced them) and
  are not ported. The `display` mode of the photo content (inert solid look
  for the offscreen pictures) IS ported.

### 1.4 Discrepancies found in the original, and how they were settled

These were found while reading the original. **D1 to D5 and D8 were fixed in
the Godot game itself on 2026-09-04**, before this port started, so the two
engines agree rather than diverge: the Unity build reproduces the FIXED
original, and the tests below exist in both.

| # | What was wrong in the original | Settled |
|---|--------------------------------|---------|
| D1 | The roll of the held photo turned the HUD picture clockwise (a 2D Control, y down, so `+roll * 90 deg` reads clockwise) while the placement anchor turned the world counter-clockwise (`+roll * 90 deg` about the placer's local +z, which points BACK at the player). The two agreed at 0 and 180 deg and nowhere else, which is exactly why no level ever asked for 90 or 270, and why nobody noticed. The design audit already used the clockwise convention. | **Fixed upstream**: `PhotoPlacer.roll_basis(steps)` is now `Basis(+z, -steps * PI / 2)`, so HUD, world and audit all read clockwise. | D2 | The full-hands prompt said "reposez (clic droit)" but dropping a photo has been F since v3. | **Fixed upstream**: `Mains pleines : posez (clic gauche) ou reposez (F) votre photo`. |
| D3 | The victory screen said "Les vingt iles" while the game has had 25 levels since v6. | **Fixed upstream**: `Les vingt-cinq iles sont derriere vous.` |

| D8 | `docs/LEVELS_V2.md` section 4 still described level 13 as "La rampe celeste" while the code has "L'escalier sur l'escalier", the sky ramp having been declared dead in section 1.4 of that same document. | **Fixed upstream**: section 13 now describes the double flight and says why the ramp died. |

## 2. Writing and project conventions

Inherited from the repository and from the original, and mandatory:

- **Player-facing text in French**, without accented characters (the original
  strings have none, because a Windows console mangles them; keep them
  verbatim from section 12). Code, comments, commit messages and documentation
  in English.
- **Never an em dash** (the character U+2014) anywhere: code, docs, strings,
  commit messages. Use a hyphen, a colon or parentheses.
- C# with Unity conventions (PascalCase types and methods, `_camelCase` private
  fields). Prefer coroutines for engine-timed sequences; if `async` methods are
  used, their names take the repository-wide `a` prefix (`aFadeOut`), never an
  `Async` suffix.
- Pure logic lives in plain C# classes with no `UnityEngine.Object`
  dependency wherever possible (geometry, catalog, capture core, decompose,
  rewind history, game state), so EditMode tests can exercise it without a
  scene. MonoBehaviours are thin adapters.
- Data is data: photos and levels are loaded from `docs/data/*.json` (copied
  into `Assets/Resources/Data/`), never transcribed by hand into C#. A test
  checks that the two copies are identical.
- No binary asset committed: no imported mesh, texture, font file, audio, or
  sprite. Built-in primitive meshes, generated meshes, generated textures,
  ShaderLab text shaders and the default TextMeshPro font are allowed.
- Every destruction during a level goes through `Rewind.Retire`, never through
  `Destroy` (section 10).

## 3. Game loop, winning and losing

1. The player appears on an island (spawn point, initial yaw), sees the level
   banner ("Niveau N : name" plus the subtitle) and the teleporter with its
   requirement ("0 / 2 piles").
2. They explore, pick up a photo (E), raise it to read it (right click), turn
   it (wheel), and place it (left click). The content becomes solid where the
   picture showed it; everything caught in the frame is replaced.
3. They collect batteries (E), insert them into the teleporter (E), and leave
   (E again once the teleporter shows "PRET"). A 0.6 s fade to black, then the
   next level.
4. After level 25, the victory screen. Enter replays from level 1.

Undo and failure:

- **R held** rewinds time (section 10): placements are undone, photos come
  back to hand, batteries return to the floor, the player walks backwards.
  Releasing R makes that instant the new present.
- **Falling below the level's `kill_y`** is the only way to lose, and it costs
  the whole level: the level is rebuilt from its definition (placements gone,
  consumed photos back, carried batteries back in the world), with the toast
  `Chute : le niveau recommence a zero.`
- **Escape** pauses (menu with level grid). **Enter** in the pause menu
  restarts the run from level 1.

Progression: the furthest level ever reached is persisted; any reached level
can be started from the level grid of the title, pause and victory menus.

## 4. Coordinate systems and units

### 4.1 Design space

All authored numbers (levels, photo props, aim points, marker positions) are
expressed in **design space**, the Godot convention of the original:

- x right, y up, **-z forward**. Right-handed.
- World: platform tops sit at y = 0 unless a level says otherwise; the spawn
  faces -z at `spawn_yaw = 0` (every level uses 0).
- Photo space (props of a photo): origin at the eye of the placer, x right,
  y up, -z forward. A prop at `pos.z = -5` stands 5 m in front of the eye.

The JSON files keep design space so that they stay diffable against the Godot
source and so that every number of the design audit and of this document stays
true as written.

### 4.2 Unity space and the one conversion

Unity is left-handed with +z forward. The only correct conversion between the
two is a mirror on z, applied in exactly one place (`DesignSpace.ToUnity`):

```
unity_position = (x, y, -z)          for world positions and photo-space props
unity_size     = (sx, sy, sz)        sizes are unchanged
unity_yaw_deg  = -yaw_rad * 180 / pi for spawn_yaw (all levels use 0)
```

Consequences to keep in mind:

- The camera's local forward is +z in Unity. A photo prop at design
  `(0, -1.72, -5)` becomes camera-local `(0, -1.72, +5)`.
- Every rotation sign flips under a mirror. Pitching the camera "down" is a
  positive x rotation in Unity and a negative one in Godot; a positive z
  rotation is counter-clockwise on screen in Unity (both in 3D and in the UI)
  and clockwise in a Godot 2D Control. Never port a rotation by copying its
  sign: port the on-screen behavior described in this document.
- The sun direction of section 14.3 and the stairs ramp of section 6.6 are
  given geometrically for this reason.

### 4.3 Physics and time

- Gravity **14 m/s^2** downward (`Physics.gravity = (0, -14, 0)`), not 9.81.
- Fixed step **60 Hz** (`Time.fixedDeltaTime = 1/60`). Player movement, rewind
  sampling and rewind playback run on the fixed clock so the history means the
  same thing at any frame rate.
- Player collider: capsule radius 0.35, height 1.75, centered 0.875 above the
  feet. The player's transform origin is at the feet. Slope limit 45 deg
  (Godot's default `floor_max_angle`).

## 5. World model

### 5.1 The ground language (grey stays, pale goes)

A rule the player reads in the color, never in words:

- **Grey is permanent.** A photo placed over grey ground or grey decor is
  ADDED to it: the slab stays, the bridge lands on top. Grey blocks are
  photographable (a picture must show what was framed) but never carved.
- **Pale and lavender are ephemeral.** The frame carves them: lavender walls
  and crates, and the rare "soft" pale ground. These blocks are photographable
  AND carvable.
- **Steel and lead are grey pushed one step further.** A steel cage
  (`sealed: true`) is never broken by any placement; a leaden battery
  (`sealed_batteries`) is never printed by the film. Both are darker greys and
  the leaden battery is inert (no bob, no spin) next to the live amber ones.

Two registries separate two unrelated questions:

- `photographable` : every block of the world, grey included. The camera
  reads it.
- `carvable` : the ephemeral half only. Placement reads it.

### 5.2 Blocks (`ErasableBlock`)

Every box of the world is an `ErasableBlock`: platforms, decor, lavender
blocks, placed photo content, painted backdrops, fragments. Fields:

| field | meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `size` | full extents |
| `colorKey` | palette key (section 14.1) |
| `emissive` | emission energy (0.25 for lavender, 0 otherwise) |
| `extraGroups` | additional registry names (`platform`, `erasable`) |
| `showMesh` | false for the invisible stairs ramp; fragments inherit it |
| `carvable` | the ground language flag; fragments inherit it |

`Create(size, color, emissive = 0, extraGroups = [], canCarve = true)`.

On enable the block joins `photographable`, joins `carvable` when `carvable`
is true, joins each of `extraGroups`, gets a box collider of `size` on the
World layer, and (when `showMesh`) a box mesh with `Materials.Solid(colorKey,
emissive)`.

**Carving** (`CarveWithFrustum(anchor, fovDeg, aspect, depth) -> bool`):

1. Sample the block's own volume on a grid in its local space: per axis,
   `steps = clamp(ceil(dim / 0.15) + 1, 2, 36)` points from `-size/2` to
   `+size/2` (spacing `dim / (steps - 1)`). Sampling in local space is what
   makes rotated blocks (the stairs ramp) work.
2. Transform each sample to anchor space and test `PhotoMath.PointInFrustum`
   with near 0 and far `depth`. Keep the local-space AABB of the hits. No hit:
   return false, nothing changes.
3. Grow the hole AABB by `0.51 * spacing` on each side (so no film thinner
   than the sampling grid survives on the hole borders).
4. `Decompose(size, holeMin, holeMax)` (pure, static, unit tested): the box
   minus the hole AABB, as at most six slabs: left and right of the hole (full
   height and thickness), below and above (within the hole's x span), in front
   and behind (within the hole's x and y span). Slabs thinner than
   `MIN_FRAGMENT = 0.08` on any axis are dropped. The hole is clamped to the
   box first, so a hole entirely off the box yields the whole box as one piece.
5. Spawn each slab as a new `ErasableBlock` with the same color, emissive,
   extra groups, `showMesh` and `carvable`, positioned at
   `blockTransform * slabCenter` (same rotation as the parent block), under the
   same parent; `Rewind.NoticeSpawn` each. `Rewind.Retire` the original.
   Return true.

The hole is the AABB of a sampled intersection, so a photo placed at an angle
over-approximates the hole. That errs on the generous side for the player and
is accepted. Fragments have no skirt (section 5.8).

Constants: `SAMPLE_SPACING = 0.15`, `MAX_STEPS = 36`, `MIN_FRAGMENT = 0.08`.

### 5.3 Cages

Level data: `{pos, size, erasable?, roof?, sealed?}`. `pos` is the center of
the cage FLOOR; the body sits at `pos + (0, size.y / 2, 0)` (its origin is the
volumetric center, which is also the point the breakable test uses).

- Groups: always `cage`; `breakable` unless `sealed`.
- Metadata: `cageSize = size`, `cageColor`.
- Color: `erasable` and not sealed -> `erasable` with emission 0.25 (lavender);
  `sealed` -> `sealed` (steel grey); otherwise `battery_tip` (dark).
- Collision (one single static body, World layer): four full thin walls,
  0.12 thick, inset 0.06 from each face, full height; plus, when `roof`, a
  roof slab 0.12 thick at the top. Neither the player nor the interaction ray
  passes between the bars.
- Visuals per wall: `barCount = max(int(span / 0.5), 2)`, then `barCount + 1`
  bars of `0.07 x height x 0.07` evenly spaced along the face, and two rails
  `span x 0.1 x 0.1` at `+-(height / 2 - 0.06)`. Roof visual: a slab
  `size.x x 0.1 x size.z` at `size.y / 2 - 0.05`.
- A lavender cage and a dark cage are the same object: any placement whose
  frustum catches the body center breaks it whole (section 6.5). The tint is
  lore. A steel cage is not in `breakable` and survives everything; the lens
  still reaches through its bars (section 7.3).
- A cage is never photographed as a volume (section 7.3).

### 5.4 Batteries

`Battery.Setup(isSealed)`. Origin at the BASE (levels place them on floors).

- Groups: `battery`; plus `copyable_battery` when not sealed (the only group
  the camera reads).
- Visual: body cylinder radius 0.16, height 0.5, at y 0.35; tip cylinder
  radius 0.06, height 0.08, at y 0.64. Live: body `battery` with emission 0.8,
  tip `battery_tip`. Leaden: body `battery_sealed`, tip `sealed_dark`.
- Interaction trigger: sphere radius 0.55 at y 0.35, Interact layer.
- Idle motion (live only): `phase += dt * 2.0`, visual y `= 0.08 + sin(phase)
  * 0.06`, spin `+= dt * 1.2` around y. Initial phase
  `= (x * 1.7 + z * 2.3) mod 2 pi` so neighbours are out of step. A leaden
  battery is inert.
- `Interact`: `GameState.CollectBattery(sealed)`, then retire the rigid shell
  if the parent is a rigidbody (a placed battery), else retire the battery.
- Prompt: `E : ramasser la pile` / `E : ramasser la pile plombee`.

A battery that comes out of a photo is ALWAYS leaden (section 7.4).

### 5.5 Photo items

`PhotoItem.Setup(defId)`. Origin at the center of the frame. Levels place
them 0.9 m above the ground; they hover on purpose (art direction, and the
design audit tolerates up to 1.7 m for hovering pickups).

- Group `photo_item`.
- Visual: frame box `0.72 x 0.82 x 0.035` in `frame`; two picture quads
  `0.66 x 0.76`, unlit, textured with `PhotoSnaps.GetTexture(defId)`, at local
  z `+0.019` and `-0.019` (the back one turned 180 deg) so the picture reads
  from both sides. The texture is re-polled every 0.5 s and adopted when the
  rendered picture lands after the item was built.
- Trigger sphere radius 0.6, Interact layer.
- Idle motion: `phase += dt * 1.6`, visual y `= sin(phase) * 0.07`, spin
  `+= dt * 0.8`. Initial phase `= (x * 2.1 + z * 1.3) mod 2 pi`.
- `Interact`: if `placer.Hold(defId)` succeeds, retire self.
- Prompt: `E : prendre la photo « <title> »` when hands are empty; otherwise
  `Mains pleines : posez (clic gauche) ou reposez (F) votre photo` (D2).

### 5.6 Camera item

`CameraItem.Setup(films)`. Origin at the base of the pedestal it floats over.

- Group `camera_item`.
- Visual: body box `0.55 x 0.34 x 0.28` in `battery_tip` at y 0.55; lens
  cylinder radius 0.11 height 0.14 in `teal` with emission 0.4, laid along the
  forward axis at `(0, 0.55, 0.2 forward)`; flash box `0.12 x 0.08 x 0.1` in
  `accent` at `(0.18, 0.76, 0)`.
- Trigger sphere radius 0.6 at y 0.55.
- Idle motion: `phase += dt * 1.8`, y `= 0.06 + sin(phase) * 0.05`, spin
  `+= dt * 1.0`. Initial phase `= (x * 1.3 + z * 2.7) mod 2 pi`.
- `Interact`: `GameState.AddFilms(films)`, retire self.
- Prompt: `E : prendre l'appareil photo (pellicule : <films>)`.

### 5.7 Teleporter

`Teleporter.Setup(required)`. Origin at the center of the pad, on the floor.
Group `teleporter`. Never carved, never photographed, never broken.

- Pad: cylinder top radius 1.3, bottom radius 1.5, height 0.22, at y 0.11,
  `teleporter`.
- Ring: torus inner radius 1.05, outer radius 1.25 (generated mesh), at
  y 2.3, `teleporter_ring` with emission 0.15 (2.0 once charged).
- Pillar: static collider box `0.4 x 1.4 x 0.4` centered `(1.9, 0.7, 0)`,
  mesh in `stone`, World layer.
- Cells: one per required battery, box `0.44 x 0.18 x 0.44` at
  `(1.9, 1.55 + i * 0.24, 0)`; `battery_tip` when empty, `battery` with
  emission 1.2 when filled.
- Label: billboard 3D text at y 3.2, font size 64, world scale 0.006 per
  pixel, outline 12: `<inserted> / <required> piles`, or `PRET` tinted
  `teleporter_ring` once charged.
- Trigger sphere radius 1.8 at y 1.2.
- Animation: `spin += dt * (charged ? 2.2 : 0.5)`; ring yaw = spin; ring
  y `= 2.3 + (charged ? sin(spin * 1.5) * 0.12 : 0)`.
- `Interact`: if `GameState.InsertBatteries() > 0` stop there; else if
  `CanTeleport` raise `DepartRequested`.
- Prompt: `E : se teleporter` when charged; `E : inserer les piles` when the
  player carries batteries; else `Il manque <n> pile` with an `s` when n > 1.

### 5.8 Platforms, decor, lavender, islands

Built by `LevelBuilder.Build(root, levelDef) -> Teleporter` from the level
definition, in this order: platforms, decor, erasables, cages, photos,
batteries, sealed batteries, camera, decor islands, teleporter. Everything a
level owns lives under `root`; unloading a level destroys `root`'s children
(placed content included).

- **Platform** `{pos, size, soft?}`: `ErasableBlock.Create(size, soft ?
  "platform_soft" : "platform", 0, ["platform"] + (soft ? ["erasable"] : []),
  canCarve: soft)`. A **skirt** mesh, child of the block (it vanishes with the
  block; fragments have none): box `(size.x * 0.9, size.y * 1.6, size.z *
  0.9)` at local `(0, -size.y * 1.1, 0)`, material `platform_side` or
  `platform_soft_side`.
- **Decor** `{pos, size, color = "wood"}`: `ErasableBlock.Create(size, color,
  0, [], canCarve: false)`. Permanent. Thin tinted slabs (`teal`, `accent`,
  size.y <= 0.2) are the placement markers; `battery_tip` slabs build showcase
  niches. A marker may carry `photo`, `aim` and `roll` (section 13.5); these
  fields are for the audit and change nothing in the world.
- **Erasable** `{pos, size}`: `ErasableBlock.Create(size, "erasable", 0.25,
  ["erasable"])`, carvable. The `erasable` group is a lavender marker for
  readability and tests only.
- **Decor islands**: six fixed boxes around every level for depth, mesh only,
  no collision, not photographable: body in `platform_side`, top slab
  `(size.x * 1.05, 0.3, size.z * 1.05)` at `pos.y + size.y / 2 + 0.1` in
  `platform`. Positions and sizes are in `levels.json` under `decor_islands`.

## 6. The photo mechanic

### 6.1 Photo definition

A photo is pure data: `{title, hint, props[], backdrop, erase_depth, seal?}`.

- `props`: list of `{kind, pos, size, color, loose?, id?}` in photo space
  (section 4.1). Kinds and the meaning of `pos`:

| kind | `pos` is | notes |
|------|----------|-------|
| `box` | center | `loose: true` makes it a falling rigid body at placement |
| `cylinder` | center | radius `size.x / 2`, height `size.y`; static |
| `battery` | the base | materializes a real, leaden, physical battery |
| `bridge` | center of the deck | deck plus two rails |
| `stairs` | front-bottom-center of the flight | `size = (width, total rise, total run)`, rises away from the camera |
| `arch` | bottom-center of the portal | two columns and a lintel |
| `photo` | center of a pickable photo item | `id` names the nested definition (photo in photo) |

- `backdrop`: `{depth, top, bottom}` or `{}`. A solid painted wall at `depth`,
  filling the frustum cross-section there, gradient from `top` to `bottom`.
  Every catalog photo has one, except a photo sealed by its own content
  (`seal: "content"`, the door).
- `erase_depth`: how deep the placement replaces. Default 12 when absent.
- Since v6.1 the backdrop stands FAR behind the carve (more than 2.5 times
  `erase_depth`): it is the horizon of the picture, not a lid on the carve. A
  carve is therefore left open, unless the photo's content seals it (the door).

### 6.2 Prop expansion

`ExpandProp(prop) -> primitives`, where a primitive is `{kind: box | cylinder
| battery | photo_item, center, size, color, loose?, photo_id?}`. The same
expansion feeds the computed thumbnail, the offscreen picture and the 3D
materialization, so what the picture shows is what gets built.

- `box`, `cylinder`: one primitive, `center = pos`; a `box` propagates
  `loose`.
- `battery`: `{battery, center = pos + (0, 0.35, 0), size (0.36, 0.7, 0.36),
  color battery}`.
- `photo`: `{photo_item, center = pos, size (0.72, 0.82, 0.06), color frame,
  photo_id = id}`.
- `bridge`: the deck `{box, pos, size, color}` plus two rails `0.1 x 0.8 x
  size.z` in `wood_dark` at `x = pos.x -+ (size.x / 2 - 0.05)`,
  `y = pos.y + size.y / 2 + 0.4`.
- `stairs`: 8 steps. `stepH = size.y / 8`, `stepD = size.z / 8`; step i
  (0-based) is a box of size `(size.x, (i + 1) * stepH, stepD)` centered at
  `(pos.x, pos.y + (i + 1) * stepH / 2, pos.z - (i + 0.5) * stepD)`. Steps
  never propagate `loose`.
- `arch`: columns `0.5 x (size.y - 0.4) x size.z` at `x = pos.x -+ (size.x / 2
  - 0.25)`, bottom at `pos.y`; lintel `size.x x 0.4 x size.z` centered at
  `pos.y + size.y - 0.2`.
- Unknown kind: warning, no primitive.

`ExpandProps(def)` concatenates over `props`.

### 6.4 Placement anchor and roll

The **placer** is a child of the player camera with an identity local
transform, rolled about the view axis by the held photo's roll. Its world
transform IS the placement anchor: position of the eye, full yaw and pitch of
the camera, plus the roll. No levelling correction: placing while looking down
lays a tilted bridge, and that is a tool.

**Roll** (`rollSteps`, 0 to 3): the mouse wheel turns the held photo by 90 deg
steps; wheel down is `+1` (`rotate_photo_cw`), wheel up `-1`; kept modulo 4;
reset to 0 on hold, on place, on drop, on clear.

Contract, per D1: **one step turns the picture 90 deg clockwise as seen by the
player**, on the HUD picture and in the world. In Unity, where a positive z
rotation is counter-clockwise on screen, that is a local rotation of
`-90 * rollSteps` degrees about the camera's forward axis for the placer, and
`-90 * rollSteps` degrees for the HUD picture's RectTransform. A unit test
pins it: with the corniche held at one step, the slab primitive's placed
center must be at camera-local `(-0.6, -1.6, forward 3.2)` (its design-space
`(1.6, -0.6)` offset turned clockwise), and at two steps at `(-1.6, +0.6)`.

The design consequence to keep: a flat slab turned 90 deg becomes a vertical
panel; the puzzles use the 180 deg flip, which keeps slabs horizontal and
inverts their offset (a slab low in the frame goes high, a slab on the right
goes left). 90 and 270 are legal, no level requires them.

### 6.5 Placement is total replacement

`PhotoPlacer.Place() -> bool` (false when nothing is held):

1. `anchor = placer world transform`; `depth = def.erase_depth` (default 12).
2. **Pass 1, carve**: for every block in a snapshot of `carvable`, call
   `CarveWithFrustum(anchor, 50, 1, depth)`. Fragments spawned during the pass
   are not revisited (the list is a snapshot).
3. **Pass 2, break**: for every body in a snapshot of `breakable`, if its
   origin (the cage's volumetric center) is inside the frustum (near 0, far
   `depth`), `Rewind.Retire` it: the whole cage disappears.
4. **Instantiate** the solid content (section 6.6) under the level root, at
   `anchor`, and `Rewind.NoticeSpawn` it.
5. Clear the hand: `heldId = ""`, `raised = false`, roll 0.

Never replaced: the teleporter, batteries, photo items, the camera item, the
player, loose rigid bodies (crates and placed batteries are rigid bodies, not
blocks, so a stack of crates survives the next placement). Placing is always
allowed, anywhere, into the void included.

An **empty photo** (no props, backdrop only) does exactly what the title says:
it pierces walls, soft floors and cages up to its depth, and paints its sky
far behind.

### 6.6 Materialization (`PhotoContent`)

`PhotoContent.Setup(def, display = false)`. Everything is expressed in the
content node's local space; the placer sets the node's world transform to the
anchor. Solid mode joins the group `placed_content`. Display mode (used by the
offscreen pictures) builds meshes only: no colliders, no groups, no pickups,
no rigid bodies.

Per primitive, solid mode:

- **box, not loose**: `ErasableBlock.Create(size, color, 0, [], canCarve:
  color == "erasable")` at `center`. Placed content joins the permanent half of
  the ground language unless the photo painted it lavender on purpose (a shot
  of a lavender plank stays carvable).
- **box, loose**: a `Rigidbody` (World layer, colliding with World) with a box
  collider and the mesh, `mass = clamp(volume * 2.0, 1.0, 10.0)`,
  `Rewind.TrackBody`. Placed upside down or over a gap, it falls. That is the
  point.
- **cylinder**: static collider (radius `size.x / 2`, height `size.y`) and
  mesh.
- **battery**: ALWAYS physical. A `Rigidbody` shell (mass 1.5, box collider
  `0.36 x 0.72 x 0.36`) at `center`, carrying a `Battery` set up **sealed**
  (leaden) at local `(0, -0.36, 0)`; `Rewind.TrackBody`. See section 7.4.
- **photo_item**: a real, pickable `PhotoItem` set up with `photo_id`, at
  `center`.
- **stairs**: the eight step boxes are MESH ONLY (no collision; a character
  cannot climb eight tiny boxes). Collision comes from one invisible
  **ramp**: an `ErasableBlock` with `showMesh = false`, not carvable, of size
  `(size.x, 0.4, sqrt(size.y^2 + size.z^2))` (2.2 x 0.4 x 8.38 for the
  catalog flight), placed so that its top face contains the hypotenuse from
  the foot of the flight, photo space `(pos.x, pos.y, pos.z)`, to the top-back
  corner `(pos.x, pos.y + size.y, pos.z - size.z)`. Its center is 0.2 m below
  the midpoint of that segment, along the segment's normal. The ramp runs
  through the inner corners of the steps; the step lips protrude above it
  (D5). Being an `ErasableBlock` whose carve samples in local space, the
  ramp's rotation is handled by carving.
- **backdrop**: a thin CARVABLE `ErasableBlock` of size `(w, h, 0.2)` where
  `(w, h) = PhotoMath.BackdropSize(depth, 50, 1)`, color key `top`, centered at
  `depth` straight ahead; a gradient quad (`Materials.Backdrop(top, bottom)`,
  unlit) of size `(w, h)` hugs its front face at local 0.101 toward the
  camera. The painted wall is the most ephemeral thing in the world: the next
  photo pierces it. Carved fragments fall back to the block's flat `top`
  color (accepted).

Display mode builds the same meshes (boxes, cylinders, a battery cylinder
radius 0.16 height 0.55 in `battery` with emission 0.8, a frame box for photo
items, the gradient quad at `depth` with no block behind it).

### 6.7 The seal rule (the door)

A carve that must be closed is closed by content that fills the frustum
cross-section exactly, with no gap. The door is the only case: its panels
pave the whole 5.6 x 5.6 section at 6 m except the opening, so its carve
(7.4 m, 1.4 m past the wall so the ground continues behind the doorway) is
capped by its own wall. It has **no painted backdrop**, and must not: a
backdrop is a solid wall, and one behind the opening would plug the doorway
(it did once, at 6.2 m). The unit test `porte_seal` samples the section at
6 m on a 21 x 21 grid: every point outside the opening (`|x| < 0.8 and
y < 0.98`) must be covered by a `frame` panel (tolerance 0.02).

### 6.8 Hold, raise, rotate, drop

- One photo in hand at a time. `Hold(id) -> bool` fails when the hand is full
  or the id is unknown; sets roll 0.
- **Right click** with a photo in hand: `RaiseToggle()`. Raised, the HUD shows
  the picture over the exact screen region the placement frustum covers,
  rotated by the roll (section 12.2). There is no 3D preview: the picture is
  all the player sees before placing. Placing while raised lowers it.
- **Wheel**: `RotateHeld(+-1)`.
- **F**: `Drop() -> bool`: spawn a `PhotoItem` of the held id under the level
  root at `eye + flatForward * 1.4 - (0, 0.4, 0)` (flat forward = camera
  forward with y zeroed, normalized; world forward if degenerate),
  `Rewind.NoticeSpawn`, clear the hand. Nothing is ever lost.
- `HeldTitle()` returns the definition title or "".
- `RestoreHeld(id, roll)` (rewind): sets the hand and roll; lowers the photo
  if the id changed.
- Photos and placed content belong to their level: loading a level drops the
  held photo and destroys everything under the level root.

### 6.9 Pictures: rendered snaps and computed thumbnails

Two pictures of a photo, from the same definition:

**Computed thumbnail** (`PhotoDefs.Thumbnail(id) -> Texture2D`, pure,
deterministic, cached, the fallback under headless runs):

- 256 x 256, filled with `frame`; inner picture rect `x 24, y 16 (from the
  top), 208 x 208` painted with the backdrop gradient (`top` at the top row to
  `bottom` at the bottom row; defaults `sky_top` / `sky_horizon` when the
  photo has no backdrop).
- Primitives sorted back to front (most negative z first). For each: project
  the eight corners with `PhotoMath.ProjectPoint` (skip corners with
  `z > -0.05`), take the normalized bounding rect, map `[-1, 1]` to the inner
  rect (y up in photo space is up in the picture), intersect with the inner
  rect, fill with the palette color darkened by `clamp(-center.z / 16, 0,
  0.35)`. A `battery` primitive gets its top 20 percent in `battery_tip`; a
  `photo_item` is a `frame` rect with an inset (one sixth) in `photo_back`
  darkened the same way.
- Unity's `Texture2D` rows go bottom-up: flip the row index when porting.

**Rendered snap** (`PhotoSnaps`, section 15.6): the display-mode content is
rendered offscreen by a square camera with the placement fov (50 deg
vertical, aspect 1, near 0.05) on the game's own sky, at 512 x 512, then the
polaroid border is painted straight onto the pixels: 24 px on the left, right
and top, 48 px at the bottom, in `frame`. The picture is therefore the exact
view the solid content will produce once placed; that identity is what makes
the raised picture blend into the world at the moment of placement.

`PhotoSnaps.GetTexture(id)` returns the rendered snap when ready, else the
computed thumbnail. `PhotoSnaps.Request(id)` queues a render (all catalog ids
at boot, each shot when taken). Under a headless run (no graphics device) the
snaps never render and the thumbnail is served, exactly like the original
under `--headless`.

## 7. The in-game camera

### 7.1 Film, viewfinder, shutter

- `GameState.cameraFilms` starts at 0 every level; picking up the camera item
  adds its films. The HUD shows the film line only when films > 0.
- **Right click with empty hands** toggles the **viewfinder**
  (`Player.ToggleViewfinder() -> bool`), possible only with empty hands and
  films left; otherwise it turns off. The HUD dims everything outside the
  exact square the shot will keep and draws corner brackets (section 12.2).
- **Left click with empty hands** is the shutter (`Player.CapturePhoto() ->
  bool`), and it answers ONLY through the viewfinder: refused (no film spent)
  when the hand is full, when films are 0, or when the viewfinder is down.
  Accepted: capture (7.2), close the viewfinder, spend one film, register the
  definition as a dynamic photo (`cliche_N`), request its snap, hold it, and
  raise it (a fresh polaroid comes out raised).
- The viewfinder closes by itself on shutter, on respawn, and whenever the
  hand fills or the film runs out (checked every fixed step).
- **The film always captures something**: framing nothing but the sky is not
  a failure. The empty photo is a tool (it pierces 12 m deep), and the film is
  always consumed.

### 7.2 Capture geometry (pure core, unit tested)

Constants: `CAPTURE_DEPTH = 12`, `CAPTURE_NEAR = 0.5`, `MAX_PROPS = 32`,
`LOOSE_MAX_EXTENT = 1.6`. Frustum: fov 50, aspect 1.

`ClipToFrustum(size, toCamera, far = 12) -> {center, size} | empty`: the part
of a box (its own space, origin at its center, `toCamera` maps it to camera
space) that lies inside the capture frustum. Analytic, two cases, because
fidelity is what matters (a photo must show objects at their real place and
real size):

- all eight corners are inside the frustum (near 0.5, far): the box is kept
  AS IT IS, `center = toCamera * 0`, `size` unchanged;
- otherwise: take the camera-space AABB of the corners; clamp its depth range
  to `[-far, -0.5]` (empty if nothing remains); clamp x and y to the frame
  half extent taken at the DEEPEST kept point (`half = HalfExtentAt(-lo.z)`),
  x within `+-half * aspect`, y within `+-half` (empty if nothing remains);
  return the clipped AABB. This is the case of the ground under the player:
  a 12 m platform whose center is behind the camera is captured as the slab of
  ground actually in frame, which is exactly what a placement of the shot
  would carve away. The copy puts back what the placement removes.

`PropsFrom(candidates, anchor) -> props`: candidates are `{kind: "box",
xform (world), size, color}` and `{kind: "battery", pos (base)}`.

- A battery is a point: kept if its visual center `base + (0, 0.35, 0)` is in
  the frustum; emitted as `{battery, pos = anchor^-1 * base, size 0, color
  battery}`.
- A box is clipped: `piece = ClipToFrustum(size, anchor^-1 * xform)`; emitted
  as `{box, pos = piece.center, size = piece.size, color}`. `loose: true`
  when the ORIGINAL size is at most 1.6 on every axis (a corner of ground is
  still ground; a 1.5 m crate falls). Accepted approximation: a clipped piece
  comes back axis-aligned in camera space, so a wall shot at an angle comes
  back facing the player.
- Sort by increasing depth (closest first), cap at 32 (the farthest dropped).

`Capture(anchor, world) -> def`: candidates are every `photographable` block
(xform, size, colorKey) and every `copyable_battery` (base position). Cages
are deliberately absent (7.3). Always returns a valid definition:

```
{ title: "Cliche", hint: "Une photo prise sur le vif. Se pose comme les autres.",
  props: PropsFrom(...), backdrop: {depth: 36.0, top: sky_top, bottom: sky_horizon},
  erase_depth: 12.0 }
```

Dynamic definitions are registered as `cliche_1`, `cliche_2`... and purged
when a level loads (`ClearDynamic`, which also drops their cached
thumbnails). They never appear in `AllIds()` (the static catalog).

### 7.3 What the film sees and does not see

- Everything the eye sees is photographable, grey ground included. Whether a
  block can be CARVED is a different question, answered at placement by the
  ground language.
- The test is geometric: bars, walls, glass and distance up to 12 m do not
  block the shot. That is how a steel cage is beaten (levels 19, 21, 24, 25):
  the lens reaches through the bars even though no placement ever breaks them.
- **A cage is never photographed.** Bars are a lattice, not a volume; copied
  as a solid block, a cage would seal its own copied battery inside.
- **A leaden battery leaves no trace on the film**: it is not in
  `copyable_battery`.
- Capture is a copy: the originals stay. The fundamental trap, taught by
  level 17: PHOTOGRAPHING copies, PLACING replaces. Placing a copy while facing
  its original destroys the original.
- Placed copies are ordinary blocks: carvable (unless grey), photographable
  again. Loose boxes and batteries become rigid bodies and fall.

### 7.4 Film does not print film

A battery that comes out of ANY photo (catalog `pile`, `coffret` chain, or a
shot) is **leaden**: worth exactly one battery to a teleporter, and never a
subject again. Without this rule a copy is itself a model: C batteries and F
films give C x 2^F batteries, and no teleporter requirement means anything. A
film yields at most one copy per copyable battery framed.

Lead stays lead through the hands: `GameState.carriedSealed` counts the leaden
ones carried; `DropBattery()` puts lead down FIRST and `InsertBatteries()`
spends it first (so the copyable one stays in hand as long as possible, and a
picked up and put down leaden battery never launders into a copyable one).

## 8. Player controller and interaction

Constants: `WALK_SPEED 5`, `SPRINT_SPEED 8`, `ACCEL 12`, `JUMP_VELOCITY 6.5`,
gravity 14, `MOUSE_SENSITIVITY 0.0022` rad per pixel, `INTERACT_RANGE 1.7`,
`EYE_HEIGHT 1.62`, camera vertical fov 75 deg, near 0.05, far 400, pitch
clamped to `+-1.45` rad.

- Look: yaw on the body from mouse x, pitch on the camera from mouse y, both
  `* 0.0022`; cursor locked and hidden while playing, free in menus.
- Move (fixed step): input vector from the four move actions; direction in the
  body's yaw frame; target horizontal velocity `direction * (sprint ? 8 : 5)`;
  horizontal velocity lerps toward the target by `min(12 * dt, 1)`; vertical
  velocity `-= 14 * dt` when airborne; jump sets it to 6.5 when grounded and
  the jump action was just pressed. Move with a `CharacterController` (slope
  limit 45, step offset 0.3, skin width 0.03).
- Derived budget (used by the levels): jump height 1.51 m, flight 0.93 s,
  horizontal reach 4.6 m walking and 7.43 m sprinting.
- **Kill plane**: when `y < kill_y` and not already fallen: cut control, zero
  velocity, raise `FellOut` once. Main rebuilds the level on the next frame
  (never destroy colliders from inside the physics step).
- **Interaction ray** (every fixed step): from the eye along the camera
  forward, 1.7 m, against World and Interact layers, hitting triggers. If the
  first hit is an Interact trigger whose owner implements `IInteractable`,
  that is the target and `interactPrompt = owner.PromptText(player)`. Solid
  world in the way blocks it (a caged battery is unreachable through the

  une pile"`. Otherwise empty.
- **E**: target present -> `target.Interact(player)`; else if carried > 0 ->

  (sealed iff `kind == "sealed"`) under the level root at `feet + flatForward
  * 1.2 + (0, 0.05, 0)`, `Rewind.NoticeSpawn`.
- **Left click**: hand full -> `placer.Place()`; else `CapturePhoto()`.
- **Right click**: hand full -> `placer.RaiseToggle()`; else
  `ToggleViewfinder()`.
- **F**: `placer.Drop()`. **Wheel down / up**: `placer.RotateHeld(+1 / -1)`.
- **R (held)**: rewind, driven by Main on the fixed clock (section 10).
- **F11**: toggle fullscreen, everywhere (menus, fades included).
- **Escape**: pause when playing. **Enter**: menus only.
- `control_enabled` false cuts input handling and movement (menus, fades,
  rewind, after a fall).
- `SetSpawn(pos, yaw, killY)` then `Respawn()`: teleport to the spawn, yaw,
  camera pitch 0, zero velocity, fallen flag off, viewfinder off.

Input actions (physical keys, so ZQSD and WASD both work):

## 10. Rewind

Holding R rewinds the level. Two tracks, because a level changes in two very
different ways.

**Motion** (continuous): sampled at `SAMPLE_HZ = 20` on the fixed clock into a
ring of at most `MAX_SAMPLES = 6000` samples (about five minutes). A sample:

```
{ t, playerPos, yaw, pitch, carried, sealed, inserted, required, films,
  heldId, rollSteps, bodies: { bodyId -> (px, py, pz, qx, qy, qz, qw) } }
```

`bodies` covers every tracked rigid body (`Rewind.TrackBody`: placed crates
and placed battery shells) that is alive and active.

**Structure** (discrete, rare): timestamped events `{t, kind: spawn |
retire, node, parent, siblingIndex}`. `Rewind.NoticeSpawn(node)` records a
creation (undoing it retires the node). `Rewind.Retire(node)` records a
destruction and performs it THE REWINDABLE WAY: the node is moved out of the
level hierarchy into a **graveyard** (an inactive holder object) and
deactivated, which takes it out of every registry and out of physics exactly
like a destroy would, but keeps it whole; its world transform and sibling
index are remembered. Undoing is putting it back. Nodes only die for good when
their event falls out of the history window (`Trim`, on every sample past the
ceiling). With no rewind instance (tooling, unit tests) `Retire` destroys.

Registries must therefore be driven by activation: a component registers in
`OnEnable` and unregisters in `OnDisable`, so a retired node leaves every
group the moment it is deactivated and rejoins them when revived.

Playback (`REWIND_SPEED = 2.5` seconds of history per second of holding):

- `StartRewind()`: cursor = now; freeze tracked bodies (kinematic).
- `StepRewind(dt)` (fixed step): `cursor = max(cursor - dt * 2.5,
  firstSampleTime)`; pop and undo every event with `t > cursor` (spawn ->
  retire; retire -> revive at its parent and sibling index, at its remembered
  transform); apply `SampleAt(samples, cursor)`: player position (lerp), yaw
  (shortest-arc lerp), pitch (lerp), zero velocity; `GameState.RestoreCounters`;
  `placer.RestoreHeld`; tracked bodies' transforms with zero velocities.
  Discrete fields (counters, held photo) come from the EARLIER sample, never
  blended: a battery is picked up at an instant, never half picked up.
- `StopRewind()`: the rewound future is gone: truncate the samples after the
  cursor, append a sample of the cursor state, `now = cursor`, unfreeze bodies.
- `AvailableSeconds()`: `(rewinding ? cursor : now) - firstSampleTime`.
- `BeginLevel()`: clear both tracks and the tracked list, destroy the
  graveyard's children for real, reset the clock, record one sample.

Pure helpers, unit tested: `Locate(samples, t)` (index of the last sample at
or before t, 0 before the history, -1 when empty) and `SampleAt(samples, t)`.

Main drives it in the fixed step: R down and menu hidden and not
transitioning -> start (if not started) and step; R up while rewinding ->
stop. Control is cut during rewind; the HUD shows the tint and
`REMBOBINAGE   <seconds> s d'historique`.

Design limit to know: a photo wasted more than five minutes ago is lost for
good; what remains is the menu or a voluntary fall.

## 11. Falling and level lifecycle (Main)

`Main` wires the systems, owns the level lifecycle and the pause flow.

- Boot: environment (section 14), `PhotoSnaps`, `Rewind` (setup with player
  and level root), player setup, menu events, title screen. With `--smoke`
  among the command line arguments attach the smoke probe; with `--shot` the
  screenshot probe (section 17).
- `LoadLevel(index)`: destroy the level root's children immediately (old
  registry members must be gone before the new level queries groups); drop the
  held photo if any, then destroy again (the dropped item belonged to the old
  level); `PhotoDefs.ClearDynamic()`; `LevelBuilder.Build`; connect the
  teleporter's `DepartRequested`; `player.SetSpawn(spawn, spawnYaw, killY)`;
  `GameState.BeginLevel(index, required)`; banner `Niveau <index + 1> :
  <name>` with the subtitle; fade in; `rewind.BeginLevel()`.
- `StartAt(index)`: refused when locked; `GameState.Reset()`; hide menu; lock
  cursor; `LoadLevel`; control on. Enter on the title starts at 0.
- Pause: control off, cursor free, pause menu. Resume: inverse.
- Depart: `transitioning = true`, control off, fade out (0.6 s); if a next
  level exists: `AdvanceLevel`, `LoadLevel`, control on; else cursor free,
  victory menu, fade in. `transitioning = false`.
- Fall (`FellOut`): deferred to the next frame; `LoadLevel(current)`, control
  on if no menu is shown, toast `Chute : le niveau recommence a zero.`
- Every frame: HUD visible iff no menu is shown; when playing and not
  transitioning, push `interactPrompt`, `SetHeld(title, texture, raised)`,
  `SetPhotoView(raised ? texture : null, rollSteps)`, `SetViewfinder(state)`;
  otherwise clear prompt, photo view and viewfinder.

Measured in the engine (gravity 14, jump 6.5, walk 5, sprint 8), relative to

Golden rules derived from the table:

- a console at 180 deg alone cannot be climbed; every puzzle that needs it
  provides the intermediate support (crate, decor block, console at 0 deg);
- no FLAT gap can be made mandatory (the sprint jump covers 7.43 m and the
  walkway deck is 8 m): a crossing that must resist the sprint needs a rise;
- the lens has no occlusion and no range other than 12 m: a climb is only a
  climb if what waits at the top is LEADEN;
- a pitched placement reaches LESS far than a level one, and falling costs
  nothing above kill_y: no dive can be made mandatory;
- the painted backdrop is a solid wall at the photo's depth: what a placement
  must reach must stand IN FRONT of that plane, or the placement seals its
  own target.

### 13.4 Level design rules baked into the data (tested)

- Landing platforms on a mandatory placement axis are at least 12 m wide, so
  side corridors survive a centered painted wall; the teleporter stays
  reachable around it.
- Every cage but steel is breakable by any placement catching its center:
  cage puzzles are economy (which photo to spend), alignment (two locks in one
  frustum, level 9) or distance (a broken cage is useless if the gap remains,
  level 19).
- Every level has at least one marker slab (`teal` standard route, `accent`
  the critical shot), a flat slab of height <= 0.2 (typically `1.4 x 0.06 x
  1.4` at y -0.01). The marker says where to stand; the direction is read
  from the level layout and the raised picture.
- Showcase niches (`battery_tip` decor) are at most 1.2 m deep and the
  battery sits within 0.6 m of the opening (interact range 1.7 m).
- At least one level hands out a camera and no catalog photo (the empty sky
  photo as the only piercing tool).
- Soft ground exists in at least one and at most five levels.
- A battery under steel requires a camera with film in the same level; a
  leaden battery is never under steel (it would be lost for good); a cage is
  never both steel and lavender; every cage declares `roof`; cages are at
  least 1.0 wide and deep.
- Steel and lead are each taught in a level where it is the only new thing
  before any level combines them (21 and 22 before 24 and 25).
- Spawn, teleporter, batteries, photos, cages and camera stand above a
  platform; `kill_y` is below the bottom of every platform.
- Subtitles are unique across levels (a repeated subtitle is a filler level).

### 14.2 Materials

`Materials.Solid(key, emissive = 0)`: cached per (key, emissive); URP Lit,
base color from the palette, roughness 0.85 (smoothness 0.15), metallic 0;
when `emissive > 0`, emission enabled with `emissionColor = color *
emissive`. `Materials.Backdrop(top, bottom)`: cached; unlit; albedo texture a
4 x 64 vertical gradient generated in code (top row `top`, bottom row
`bottom`). Photo pictures on items: unlit, textured. Lavender emission 0.25
reads as a faint shimmer, never as a light source.

### 14.3 Sky, light, fog, post

- Sky: gradient from `sky_top` at the zenith to `sky_horizon` at the horizon;
  below the horizon `sky_horizon` fading to `sky_horizon` darkened by 15
  percent at the nadir. A ShaderLab skybox shader with those four colors.
- Ambient: from the sky, at half strength (Godot: sky contribution 0.5,
  energy 0.8). In Unity: gradient ambient with sky `sky_top` at reduced
  intensity, equator `sky_horizon`, ground the darkened horizon; tune by eye
  against the reference renders.
- Sun: directional, color (1, 0.96, 0.88), intensity about 1.15, shadows on
  (4 cascades, max distance 120, soft, normal bias about 1.5). Direction in
  design space `(-0.354, -0.707, -0.612)` (45 deg down, coming from the +x,
  +z side), which is Unity Euler `(45, -30, 0)` after the mirror.
- Fog: exponential, color `sky_horizon`, density 0.0012 as a starting point
  (Godot's exponential fog and Unity's differ in formula: match the veil on
  the decor islands 30 to 40 m away by eye).
- Post: MSAA 2x, FXAA, tonemapping. The original uses Godot's Filmic; use URP
  Neutral rather than ACES, which desaturates bright albedos and would dull
  the pastel palette (a known trap in this repository's Godot projects).
- Window: 1600 x 900 default, resizable, vsync on; F11 toggles borderless
  fullscreen.

### 14.4 Look

Pastel and clean, readable without textures: cream platforms with sand
skirts, coral and teal accents, amber emissive batteries, indigo teleporter,
light blue to white sky with soft haze. Photos are white-framed polaroids.
Ephemeral objects in greyed lavender with a faint emissive shimmer; steel and
lead in darker greys, lead inert.

### 14.5 Snap studio lighting

The offscreen picture studio (section 15.6) uses the same sky, a directional
light of color (1, 0.96, 0.88) intensity 1.15 pitched 40 deg down and yawed
30 deg (Unity Euler `(40, -30, 0)`), and a slightly brighter ambient than the
game (Godot: sky contribution 0.7, energy 1.1). Camera fov 50 vertical,
aspect 1, near 0.05.

## 16. Godot to Unity conversion notes

Pitfall by pitfall, so that no one rediscovers them:

- **Handedness**: one mirror on z, in `DesignSpace`, nowhere else. Every
  rotation sign flips; port behaviors, not signs (section 4.2, D1).
- **Groups -> registries driven by OnEnable/OnDisable**, or the graveyard
  (section 10) does not take retired nodes out of the world.
- **Retire, never Destroy** during a level. `Destroy` inside the physics step
  is also what the original avoids by deferring the fall restart.
- **Area3D -> trigger collider** on the Interact layer, found by
  `Physics.Raycast(..., QueryTriggerInteraction.Collide)`. The closest hit
  wins, so world geometry in front of a trigger blocks the interaction (cage
  walls). Godot's ray hit both bodies and areas the same way.
- **CharacterBody3D -> CharacterController**: custom gravity in the fixed
  step, `isGrounded` for the jump (add a short downward probe if
  `isGrounded` flickers on slopes), slope limit 45 so the 33 deg stairs ramp
  is walkable and the 4 cm bridge lip is climbed naturally.
- **RigidBody3D freeze -> `isKinematic = true`** during rewind, then restore.
- **StaticBody3D -> collider without Rigidbody**.
- **Label3D -> TextMeshPro 3D text** with a billboard script (face the
  camera every frame).
- **SubViewport with own world -> RenderTexture camera on a dedicated layer**
  far from the level (section 15.6). Ambient light is global in Unity: accept
  it.
- **Image (y down) -> Texture2D (y up)**: flip rows in the thumbnail painter
  and in the polaroid border painter (the 48 px band is at the BOTTOM of the
  picture).
- **Godot Color constants are sRGB**: keep the numbers, keep the project in
  Linear color space, assign as `Color`. Emission = color times energy, HDR.
- **Godot fov is vertical** (as Unity's). Camera 75, photo 50.
- **Godot `rotation.y` positive turns left; Unity's turns right.** Spawn yaws
  are all 0, so only the mirror rule matters.
- **`posmod` for the roll**: C# `%` is negative for negative operands; use
  `((roll + d) % 4 + 4) % 4`.
- **Tweens -> coroutines**; `await frame_post_draw` -> `WaitForEndOfFrame`.
- **`user://` -> `PlayerPrefs`** (or `Application.persistentDataPath`).
- **Input**: physical keys through the Input System so ZQSD and WASD both
  work without a layout switch; mouse wheel as two actions (`scroll/y`
  positive and negative).
- **Fullscreen**: `Screen.fullScreenMode` toggled between
  `FullScreenWindow` and `Windowed`, handled before the menu guard so it
  works everywhere.
- **Headless runs render nothing**: the original served drawn thumbnails
  under `--headless`; do the same under `-nographics`, and make the smoke
  probe never depend on a rendered pixel.
- **Frame counts in probes are physics steps**, never render frames: a
  headless run renders far faster than real time and gravity advances on the
  fixed step only.
- **Torus and gradient textures are generated**, not imported.
- **Line endings**: write generated files with LF; a tool that rewrites a
  script in CRLF has broken validation in this repository before.

### 17.4 Design audit (port of `tools/design_audit.gd`)

Geometric and conservative: models the world as standable rectangles
(platforms, decor thicker than 0.2, lavender blocks, roofed cage tops) and
walks them from the spawn with the cinematic budget, erring on the side of the
player, so that every reported line is a real defect. Lifts: jump 1.50, one
crate 2.80, two crates 4.10, console 2.67, stairs 6.12, two flights 10.75;
reaches: jump 4.6, sprint 7.43, stairs 7.0, walkway 13.6; a camera is worth
one crate of lift and a walkway of reach; crates come from `caisse` photos
plus one per film when a crate-sized block exists; going down is free.

Codes and checks:

| code | meaning |
|------|---------|
| `VIDE` | a target (battery, lead, photo, camera, teleporter) has no surface under it |
| `FLOTTE` | a target is more than 0.5 m (1.7 m for hovering pickups) above its support |
| `HORS_ATTEINTE` | a target's support is unreachable with the level's maximum lift and reach |
| `ENCASTRE` | a target is inside a solid |
| `COLLE` | two pickups within 0.7 m fight over the interaction ray |
| `SPAWN` | the spawn is inside a solid or a steel cage |
| `REPERE` | a marker floats, is unreachable, is inside steel, names an unknown photo or aims at the player |
| `PIEGE` | a reachable surface with no way back up (tolerated when the teleporter is there) |
| `TRIVIALE` | with empty hands, sprinting, walls counted, the required batteries and the teleporter are reachable without a photo; or a battery a climb was built for is printable from free ground within 12 m; or the walkway crosses nothing wider than 7.43 m |
| `EMMURE` | a declared shot aims past its own painted wall, or that wall seals a battery behind it within its span |
| `ENTERRE` | a declared shot materializes all (or some) of its static primitives inside a permanent block |
| `REDITE` | a subtitle repeats an earlier level's |

Expected result on the shipped data: zero defects on 25 levels (verified on
the original on 2026-09-03).
