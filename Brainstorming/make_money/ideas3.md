# AI-automated businesses outside coding: a route to paying customers

Research date: 6 September 2026. Currency: EUR unless explicitly marked USD or GBP.

## Recommendation

Start with a **managed catalog preparation service for small distributors**, sold as a fixed batch of verified, import-ready product records. It has a tangible deliverable, can be demonstrated before gaining access to a client's systems, and can be delivered using existing tools. Test **quote follow-up for one local trade** if you have warm access to business owners. Test **tender qualification briefs** if you can reach firms already bidding for public contracts.

The initial business should sell completed work, not software development, AI consulting, or a promise of passive income. AI handles extraction, drafting, classification and formatting; you retain sales, quality control and exceptions. Recurring revenue becomes plausible when the buyer repeatedly sends new work.

These are recommendations for experiments, not validated businesses. No customers were interviewed or contacted for this research, and no revenue was earned. Vendor sources establish available capabilities and competing offers; they do not prove demand for a new solo provider.

Local access or specialist experience can change the ranking substantially. You need no custom application for the pilots; spreadsheets, document tools and existing automation products are sufficient.

## 1. Managed catalog preparation

**Buyer and trigger.** A homewares, hospitality-supplies or similar distributor with a new supplier range and messy PDFs/spreadsheets. Target someone who already sells products and has a catalog backlog. Begin with non-regulated goods and simple variants.

**Deliverable.** For each SKU: approved title, attributes, normalized units, category, source references, missing-data flags and a draft description. Deliver an import-ready CSV matching one agreed template, a corrections log and an exception list. Limit the pilot to 50 SKUs, at most 12 fields each, one source language and one revision.

**Workflow.** Receive authorized supplier files → extract values → map to the target schema → flag conflicts → draft descriptions from verified facts → check each required field against the source → deliver to the buyer for import. Missing values stay missing. Do not infer certification, compatibility, dimensions or performance claims.

**Why pay?** The proposed value is a cleared backlog and fewer incorrect records. Generic description writing is weak: Shopify already supplies automated translation and market adaptation, so text generation alone is easy to substitute. Its documentation also describes source updates requiring renewed translation runs. [Shopify Translate & Adapt](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/international/translate-adapt-app).

**Acquisition.** Select 20 distributors in one niche with inconsistent public product records. Prepare a private, three-row example from clearly identified public facts; use fictional records if reuse permission is unclear. Approach the catalog/e-commerce manager with the fixed batch offer. A web agency can also refer catalog work it does not want to handle.

**Economics and test.** At 750 per monthly batch, allow 50 cash delivery costs and 7 human hours. At an imputed 30/hour, that leaves 490 before acquisition and fixed overhead. Charge separately for supplier-file cleanup beyond the agreed scope. Continue only if the pilot stays within six delivery hours, all critical fields are verified, and the buyer pays for a second batch or commits a dated next batch.

**Main failure mode.** Ten incompatible supplier formats turn an apparently repetitive task into bespoke data repair. Reprice per source complexity rather than silently absorbing it. Expansion: product-image cleanup first; reviewed localization only when you have the language capability.

## 2. Managed follow-up on outstanding quotes

**Buyer and trigger.** A cleaning, landscaping or renovation business with enough existing quote volume to justify a fee, and no consistently maintained follow-up process. Start with its own active inquiries, not purchased leads.

**Offer.** Reconcile up to 60 open quotes, prepare an agreed reminder sequence, classify replies, route questions to the owner, and provide a weekly status report. The owner controls prices, discounts, scheduling promises and job acceptance.

**Workflow.** Import approved quote status → remove accepted, rejected, expired and opted-out records → draft relevant messages → send only under the client's approved rules → stop on reply → update status. Pilot messages can be manually approved before sending. Existing CRM automations should handle simple timing; AI is useful for reply classification and drafting around the customer's question.

**Competition.** Jobber already offers automatic quote follow-ups. Selling the reminder feature itself has little differentiation. The hypothesis is that some owners will pay for someone to maintain the queue and resolve exceptions. [Jobber quotes](https://www.getjobber.com/features/quotes/).

**Acquisition.** Start with three warm introductions to one trade, then approach relevant local firms individually. Ask for counts of monthly quotes, unanswered quotes and typical job contribution before proposing a pilot. Exclude prospects already happy with their existing system.

**Economics and proof.** At 450/month, budget 40 delivery costs and four human hours: 290 remains after valuing delivery time at 30/hour, before acquisition and overhead. Illustratively, two genuinely incremental jobs contributing 250 each would cover the fee; two jobs merely accepted during the pilot do not prove incrementality. Compare matched quote cohorts or alternate weeks if volume permits, and report paid jobs and contribution rather than quote face value.

**Stop condition.** No accessible quote history, insufficient volume, or a persistent support burden above four hours/month at this price. If incremental sales cannot be measured, renewal must rest on measured admin time saved and buyer satisfaction, not claimed revenue uplift.

## 3. Tender qualification briefs

**Buyer and trigger.** A cleaning, grounds-maintenance, catering or training company already bidding, with relevant references and someone able to make the bid decision.

**Offer.** A short, source-linked brief covering service fit, geography, lots, submission deadline/time zone, required documents, visit requirements and disqualifying gaps. Include a reasoned pursue/skip recommendation for the buyer to review. Cap the subscription at four briefs and an agreed screening volume; price large tender packs separately.

**Workflow.** Use official alerts → obtain the full tender documents → extract criteria with page references → compare with the client's approved capability profile → verify deadlines and mandatory conditions manually → send a brief. Monitor amendments to shortlisted opportunities within the contracted period. The client owns its bid, declarations and submission.

**Competition and evidence.** Free alerts already exist. The service must improve qualification, not repackage a feed. French government guidance points businesses toward BOAMP, European notices and buyer platforms; these sources do not establish that a client is eligible or likely to win. [Government guide for SMEs](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/files/files/Publications/2022/Guides/2022-guide-tpe-pme-marches-publics.pdf), [Service Public: BOAMP access and alerts](https://entreprendre.service-public.gouv.fr/vosdroits/R14631).

**Acquisition.** Identify firms with public awards in one niche and region. Show a sample brief using a real published opportunity, clearly marked as a sample rather than an eligibility determination. Tender advisers can be referral or white-label partners; any partner fee must enter the economics.

**Economics and stop condition.** At 350/month, allow 30 tools/data allocation and four delivery hours, leaving 200 after delivery labor. During the pilot ask the buyer to score every brief. Stop or change the niche if fewer than two of three briefs help a real bid/no-bid decision, or document review consumes the entire margin. Never promise a contract award.

## 4. Reviewed training-video localization

**Buyer.** A training provider or exporter that owns existing onboarding/product-training videos and has an identified audience in another language.

**Offer.** Translated script, subtitles, glossary and dubbed video with timing checks. Start with one language, ten source minutes, one speaker and one revision. Quote native review separately or include a confirmed reviewer allowance. Exclude safety-critical instruction at the initial stage.

**Automation and limits.** Transcription, first translation and audio alignment can be automated; a qualified speaker reviews meaning and terminology, and you check on-screen text, timing and playback. Obtain permission for footage and any use of a person's voice or likeness. If you cannot secure competent review, sell subtitle preparation in a language you can verify instead.

**Evidence.** HeyGen's published API rates distinguish audio-only translation from lip-sync modes. These support production feasibility, not the service's selling price. At the checked audio-only rate of USD 0.0167/second, 20 minutes is about USD 20.04 for one pass, before retries, other tools or review. The consumer app uses different billing. [HeyGen API pricing](https://developers.heygen.com/docs/pricing).

**Acquisition and test.** Approach five training agencies with an authorized 60-second demonstration. Test a 450 pilot; sell the 800 recurring package only with a genuine monthly content pipeline. Allow 180 cash costs including reviewer budget and six operator hours per 20-minute package, leaving 440 before acquisition/overhead. Reviewer quotes may invalidate this model. Stop if terminology correction or editing exceeds the agreed time budget.

## 5. Product-image cleanup and channel formatting

**Buyer.** A wholesaler with real product photography but inconsistent backgrounds, cropping and output sizes.

**Offer.** For 100 supplied images: consistent background, agreed crop, file naming and two export formats, with original-to-final contact sheets. Exclude complex retouching, reflective/transparent products and generative lifestyle scenes from the standard price.

**Evidence and workflow.** Photoroom supports batch product-image editing and recommends human validation when product accuracy matters. Automate background cleanup and exports, then inspect every image for changed logos, color, shape, included accessories and defects. [Photoroom e-commerce capabilities](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/e-commerce), [Photoroom documentation](https://docs.photoroom.com/).

**Selling and economics.** Reach five product photographers or e-commerce agencies as an overflow partner. A 240 pilot is 30 straightforward images; at 650/month for 100, budget 60 cash costs and five operator hours, leaving 440 before acquisition/overhead. Continue if at least 90% are accepted on first review and rework stays within the allowance. Buyers can run the tool themselves; reliability, coordination and finished files must justify the fee.

## 6. Support knowledge and exception management

**Buyer.** An established shop with repetitive delivery/returns questions, documented policies and an existing helpdesk.

**Offer.** A 600 setup sprint to classify historical tickets, repair missing knowledge and configure permitted answers. Then 500/month for weekly answer audits, up to 20 knowledge updates and an exception report. Software, usage and frontline human support are paid by the client separately; this is not unlimited outsourced support.

**Evidence.** Gorgias sells AI support with helpdesk and automation billing. Its pricing material and billing documentation describe these components differently enough that a live quote should be checked before budgeting. Do not assume the AI-resolution price is the complete cost per ticket. [Gorgias pricing](https://www.gorgias.com/pricing), [billing documentation](https://docs.gorgias.com/en-US/how-youre-billed-for-using-gorgias-199385).

**Acquisition and test.** Partner with an e-commerce agency; offer an anonymized historical-ticket audit. Start in draft mode, verify answers against policy, and route disputed refunds or policy exceptions to staff. At 500, allow 30 provider costs and five operator hours, leaving 320 before acquisition/overhead. Continue only when review shows fewer staff minutes per resolved case without increased errors or repeat contacts. If native setup works well already, the buyer may not need this service.

## 7. Interview-to-SOP packs

**Buyer.** A growing cleaning company, small distributor or administrative team onboarding staff while operational knowledge lives in the owner's head.

**Offer and workflow.** Two structured interviews plus existing materials become five short procedures, checklists and a responsibility map. AI drafts; the process owner verifies steps; one intended user attempts the procedure. Deliver in the buyer's existing document system. Avoid legal policies and specialist safety procedures in the pilot.

**Competition.** Trainual already offers AI-assisted documentation and training content. The proposed value is eliciting accurate knowledge, resolving contradictions and getting procedures tested by staff. [Trainual documentation](https://trainual.com/documentation).

**Acquisition and test.** Ask an operations consultant or small-business network for introductions to companies currently hiring. At 650, allow 30 cash costs and eight human hours, leaving 380 before acquisition/overhead. Stop if the owner cannot attend interviews or approve facts. Quarterly updates are repeat project revenue, not monthly recurring revenue. Do not force a subscription on static documents.

## 8. Competitor-change briefs

**Buyer.** A specialist distributor whose purchasing or sales manager makes recurring decisions about competing ranges, availability or promotions.

**Offer.** Monitor ten agreed public pages and produce one weekly brief: verified change, dated source, relevance to the buyer and a suggested question/action. Bundle low-significance changes instead of flooding the inbox.

**Evidence and differentiation.** Visualping already monitors pages and interprets changes with AI. A new service therefore needs niche context and reliable verification beyond generic summaries. [Visualping competitor monitoring](https://visualping.io/competitive-monitoring).

**Acquisition and test.** Prepare two historical or clearly labeled sample briefs for ten niche firms. Ask what decision each would change. At 250/month, allow 40 tool costs and three operator hours, leaving 120 before acquisition/overhead. Stop if the buyer cannot name a useful decision after a month. Dynamic pages and access restrictions can make this uneconomic; use permitted public sources and price source failures explicitly. Shared public research can support multiple clients, but their confidential strategy must remain separate.

## 9. Webinar-to-sales-material packs

**Buyer.** A training provider or B2B consultancy already recording webinars and selling a defined service.

**Offer.** One recording becomes three reviewed short clips, a source-checked article and an email draft linked to the buyer's existing offer. Include timestamps, one brand template and one revision; the client approves publication. Sell reuse of existing expertise, not guaranteed views or synthetic thought leadership.

**Evidence.** OpusClip already sells automated clipping and captions at low subscription prices. Editing alone has weak differentiation; choosing material relevant to the client's offer and preserving the speaker's meaning are the proposed value. [OpusClip pricing](https://www.opus.pro/pricing).

**Acquisition and test.** Approach ten firms with recent webinars but little follow-up material, using a timestamped sample outline before doing unpaid editing. At 600/month for two packs, allow 40 tools and five human hours, leaving 410 before acquisition/overhead. Stop if clients cannot supply recordings regularly or demand unlimited editorial revisions. Measure approved assets actually used and attributable inquiries where observable; do not equate impressions with sales.

## 10. Managed inbound reception

**Buyer.** A local service business losing routine inquiries outside office hours, with clear service territory, call-routing rules and an owner available for escalation.

**Offer.** Configure an existing AI receptionist, maintain FAQs and routing, and inspect call outcomes. Start with overflow calls only. The bot gathers contact details and routes requests; it does not diagnose emergencies or promise technical work or prices outside approved rules.

**Evidence.** Smith.ai publicly offers AI reception, qualification and scheduling, including managed setup on some plans. This creates both feasibility and competition. Its USD pricing is a reference for that provider, not proof of French-number availability or language suitability. Verify target-country support and actual French call quality before selling. [Smith.ai pricing](https://smith.ai/pricing/ai-receptionist).

**Acquisition and test.** Use a warm introduction to one trades business and review its missed-call data. At 300/month management fee with the vendor billed directly to the client, allow 20 provider costs and four operator hours, leaving 160 before acquisition/overhead. Test names, addresses, noise, interruptions, transfers and appointment conflicts. Require all critical scripted tests to pass and a viable human fallback before live use. Stop if monitoring or incident coverage cannot fit your schedule. This ranks low because availability obligations can overwhelm a part-time operator.

| Generic AI e-books, print-on-demand art, faceless video channels | Revenue depends on audience/distribution; output volume is not proof of buyers | Preorders or a relevant audience with prior purchases |
| Generic prompt packs or an undifferentiated AI newsletter | Easy substitution and uncertain retention | Repeated paid requests for the same specialist material |
| Automated dropshipping | Supplier quality, refunds, paid acquisition and working capital remain | A tested product, dependable supplier and positive contribution after returns/acquisition |

## Economics: separate revenue from owner income

Use a cash ledger and a time ledger. Count prospecting, rejected proposals, onboarding, delivery, revisions, subscriptions, failed tool runs, refunds and payment fees. The 30/hour labor value below is a decision-making assumption, not a wage payment or tax calculation.

Illustrative steady month for the catalog service; exclude one-time pilot/setup fees:

| Item | No sales | Small foothold | Repeatable delivery |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Monthly clients at 750 | 0 | 2 | 4 |
| Revenue | 0 | 1,500 | 3,000 |
| Delivery cash costs at 50/client | 0 | 100 | 200 |
| Fixed tools/admin cash allowance | 100 | 100 | 100 |
| Acquisition cash allowance | 50 | 50 | 50 |
| Cash contribution before tax/social charges | -150 | 1,250 | 2,650 |
| Delivery hours at 7/client | 0 | 14 | 28 |
| Sales/admin hours | 16 | 16 | 16 |
| Total owner hours | 16 | 30 | 44 |
| Imputed owner labor at 30/hour | 480 | 900 | 1,320 |
| Economic surplus after imputed labor | -630 | 350 | 1,330 |

This is capacity arithmetic, not a month-six forecast. It assumes usable inputs, repeat orders and no significant churn. At four clients, delivery rising from seven to fourteen hours per client adds 28 hours and removes 840 of economic surplus, leaving 490. Fixed overhead here is only a placeholder; add the actual costs of your operating setup. Figures exclude VAT, income tax, social contributions and any required insurance/accounting costs not covered by the allowance.

Customer acquisition sensitivity: if 12 selling hours plus 50 cash spending yields one customer, economic acquisition cost is 410. If the same effort yields two, it is 205 each. These are examples, not conversion-rate benchmarks. At 490 contribution after delivery labor per repeat catalog batch, acquisition could be recovered within one repeat batch under these assumptions; onboarding and fixed overhead still need coverage. If it yields zero customers, there is no payback.

Deposits are cash received before all work is earned. Record accepted deliveries, invoices, collected payments and outstanding obligations separately. A booked order is not collected revenue; a client's recovered invoice is not your revenue.

## A 30-day validation plan

**Days 1–3: define and demonstrate one offer.** Choose one distributor niche. Prepare a three-SKU example, required input checklist, 50-SKU scope, acceptance criteria and fixed price. Cap pre-sale preparation at six hours. Spend at most 100 before the first paid pilot; reserve the rest of the 300 experiment budget for delivery and corrections. No platform build, paid ads or annual tool commitments.

**Days 4–10: test the buyer problem.** Identify 20 relevant firms and seek five short conversations through warm introductions, relevant professional networks or individually researched outreach. These are activity targets, not predicted conversion rates. Ask: what is the backlog, who does it now, what goes wrong, when must it ship, and who can approve a purchase? Quote 450 for the defined batch, with 50% upfront and 50% on acceptance under an agreed scope.

For French B2B email, CNIL guidance requires relevance to the recipient's professional role, appropriate information and a simple way to object; every message must identify the sender and provide an opt-out. Consumer email has different consent requirements. Do not interpret public contact details as permission for indiscriminate bulk messaging. [CNIL email prospecting guidance](https://www.cnil.fr/la-prospection-commerciale-par-courrier-electronique).

**Days 11–17: deliver one paid pilot.** Track all operator time and actual tool costs. Keep a source reference for each factual field; verify critical values on every record. Send missing information back as exceptions. Do not import or publish until the client has approved the batch and authorized that action. Record first-pass acceptance, corrections, actual margin and whether the buyer used the files.

**Days 18–24: seek repeat payment.** Offer the 750/100-SKU package only if the customer has enough new work. If demand is seasonal, offer prepaid batches instead of inventing recurring revenue. Request permission to use a de-identified before/after example and ask for one relevant introduction.

**Days 25–30: decide from receipts and workload.** Continue when there is at least one paid, accepted pilot plus a paid repeat order or deposit, acceptable delivery time and a plausible route to more similar customers. One pilot validates only a first transaction, not a scalable business. If prospects acknowledge the pain but nobody pays, revise the buyer/offer/price before further automation. If two separately targeted groups of 20 qualified prospects produce no paid pilot, pause and investigate channel access, urgency and trust rather than generating more features.

## Delivery rules that protect the margin

1. State input limits, output count, turnaround start condition and revision allowance in every offer.
2. Use client-owned accounts where practical; confirm vendor processing terms and permission before uploading confidential material. Collect only needed inputs and agree deletion/retention.
3. Keep source facts separate from AI suggestions. Flag uncertainty instead of filling gaps with plausible text.
4. Define the exceptions that return to the buyer and the decisions the operator may make. Scheduling, publication, spending and customer commitments need explicit operating authority.
5. Measure automation through saved operator minutes and accepted results. Counting generated items hides rework.
6. Standardize only after several paid deliveries reveal what repeats. A tool subscription is not a business; the repeat purchase is the signal.

## Evidence quality and open questions

Sources were checked through web research on the date above. Prefer the linked official provider documentation and French public authorities over earnings anecdotes. Provider descriptions support capability/competition claims only; marketing outcome statistics were not adopted as revenue forecasts. Most substantive content here—buyer selection, package design, proposed prices and stop criteria—is original analysis requiring customer validation.

The highest-value unknowns are whether you can reach a buyer, obtain representative inputs, deliver within the time cap and earn a second payment. Native features, plan limits and prices can change; verify the chosen vendor's current quote and regional suitability when scoping a real pilot.
