FAQ

Fair questions.

Is this AI-generated documentation?

AI pipelines do the reading and the drafting - that's why the prices are fixed and the delivery is fast. But nothing ships on the model's word: every path and symbol is machine-checked against your repository, factual claims are spot-audited against the code, and a senior engineer reviews every deliverable. The verification report ships with the pack, so you can check us.

What happens to our code?

Your code is processed in an ephemeral workspace and never retained: we keep only extracted facts (module names, file paths, verified claims), not source. Processing is EU-based, we sign your NDA and DPA, and on request the pipeline runs inside your own CI instead of our infrastructure.

How can you commit to fixed prices on legacy code?

Two mechanisms. The Control Score sizes the terrain before anything is quoted, so we price from measurement, not hope. And nothing gets modernized or evolved without a Shield of characterization tests first - which converts "unknown risk" into "verified non-regression" and makes fixed prices survivable for both of us.

We have millions of lines across dozens of repositories. Does any of this scale?

Yes - because we don't treat an estate like a big repo. Prices are built from meters measured at intake (a base fee per repository, degressive per-1,000-line rates, per-hotspot and per-ticket meters), so a 30k-line repo and a 2M-line estate each pay for what they actually are. And estates are never quoted blind: the Estate Survey (2,900 EUR + 90 EUR per repo) triages the whole portfolio first, then deep work goes only where the risk actually lives. Documenting all 40 repos uniformly would be malpractice; prioritizing the 8 that matter is the job.

We tried writing docs before. They rotted within a year.

Hand-written docs rot because updating them is a chore nobody owns. Ours are generated from a fact graph extracted from your code, so they regenerate when the code changes - that's the Tower subscription. Documentation stops being a task and becomes a property of the system.

What's a "behavior change ticket"?

During Evolve, new requirements are expressed as edits to your system's recovered specification: behavior X becomes behavior Y, with acceptance criteria, a computed impact set, and a fixed price. You sign the ticket list before work starts, and CI guarantees nothing outside the tickets changes. You review what the system will do differently, not 4,000 lines of diff.

Which stacks do you cover?

Python and JavaScript/TypeScript are first-class today; other stacks are assessed at intake - the Control Score's feasibility check tells you (and us) honestly whether your codebase is a fit before any money changes hands.
